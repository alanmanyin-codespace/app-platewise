## Slide 2 — The Problem

Few people plan what they eat. It costs them their health, wealth and convenience.

### The evidence
- **Health:** pantry rarely stocked for goals.
- **Wealth:** 9 people threw out food monthly. Overspend and waste.
- **Convenience:** 12 people spend 15+ min a week planning what to eat. The time and stress of planning.

### The pain
Few people plan what they will eat, so the pantry is stocked without their goals in mind. It costs them health, wealth and convenience.

One shopper: “Meals that use same ingredients for a week so things go to waste less.”

### How it's solved today
Meal apps ignore what is already in the fridge. Paper flyers, Flipp, PC Optimum points, a paper list, or just walking in.

### Cost of the problem
Money thrown out, time lost every week, and a pantry that doesn't help them reach their health goals.

## Slide 3 — Early Adopters (ICP)

Busy professionals that do not have the time to plan their meals.

### The behavior test
- Orders takeout or delivery to save time.
- Already uses a savings tool: PC Optimum, Flipp, Flyers.
- Plans a list, still loses 15+ min and wastes food.

### What they already do
- 12 already spend 15+ min a week planning.
- 10 shop at 2+ stores.
- 10 already save with PC Optimum.

### Their trigger
A busy week and a half-empty pantry: they need to plan for a restock, or order takeout again.

### Where to find them
Superstore, shoppers, Instagram, LinkedIn, and Calgary workplaces and community groups.

**Customer discovery:** We asked 25 Calgary shoppers today in person. 16 want to try our app. 9 left an email to test it.

## Slide 4 — The Solution

One weekly restock, planned around your goals, pantry and budget, so you buy only what you need.

### Why and how
- **Why:** few people plan what they eat, and it costs health, wealth and convenience.
- **How:** learn their goals and preferences, buy only what they need, at the best store.
- **What:** a tailored weekly restock plan.

### Value proposition
For busy Calgary shoppers, PlateWise plans your weekly restock around your goals, pantry and budget.

1. **Know your home:** Your goals, weekly budget and pantry, set once and updated as you shop.
2. **Plan your week:** Dinners from this week's deals, your pantry and your goals, priced for two.
3. **Restock near you:** Only what's missing, at the best store near you, with trip cost. A heads-up before food goes bad.

## Slide 8 — Business Model

How does this make money, who pays, and do the unit economics work?

### Revenue model
- Now: free, focus on getting users.
- Free tier: a few light grocer deal ads.
- Premium: a few dollars a month, no ads, plus extras like pantry tracking.
- Later: grocer partners, affiliate cuts.

### Pricing
- **Free:** flyer deals, dinners, list.
- **Premium:** $3.99 a month, no ads, pantry heads-up.
- Same as Deal Dish Pro, under CartBrain's $12.

### Unit economics
Assumption, to test with our first 100 users:
- **CAC:** $0 to $2 (in-store QR, shared links).
- **LTV:** $3.99 × months on Premium.

### Key assumptions
Ads stay light and relevant, like sponsored flyer deals. Our plan: free to start, no ads while you cook. Grocers pay once we have users.

## Slide 9 — Go-To-Market Plan

How will you reach early adopters and win the first 100 customers?

### First 10
In-store interviews at Superstore and the survey QR code. Everyone who says yes gets the prototype link.

### First 100
Instagram stories and LinkedIn. Share-a-recipe links: each shared recipe shows the deal and what it saved, and links back to the app.

### Scale
Calgary community groups and shared recipe and list links. Then a pilot with one grocer partner. Metric to watch: week-2 return rate.
